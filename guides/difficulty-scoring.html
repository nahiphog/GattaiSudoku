How Gattai Sudoku Difficulty Scoring Works

Every puzzle on this site is assigned a difficulty rating based on the techniques required to solve it, not on subjective feel or solver time.

The evaluator walks through the puzzle's solution step by step, recording which technique each deduction uses and how much it contributes to the cumulative score. The final rating depends on both the most advanced technique required and the total score accumulated.

Score calculation

The solver assigns a point value to each technique based on its complexity, then sums them across the entire solve path:

  • Full House: 4 points — a cell with only one candidate remaining
  • Naked Single: 4 points — a cell whose only possible digit is clear from its house peers
  • Hidden Single: 14 points — a digit that can only go in one cell of a house, even though that cell has multiple candidates
  • Intersections: 30–60 points
  • Pairs & Triples: 80–140 points
  • Fish (X-Wing, Swordfish, Jellyfish): 150–350 points
  • Wings (XY-Wing, XYZ-Wing): 200–400 points
  • Single-Digit Patterns: 180–300 points

The evaluator always chooses the cheapest applicable technique at each step, mirroring how a human solver would naturally proceed.

How puzzles are designed

Puzzles are created by a process called rotational-pair digging:

  1. Start with a fully solved, compatible Gattai grid pair.
  2. Remove clues one rotational pair at a time (a givens and its 180° partner).
  3. After each removal, verify the puzzle still has exactly one solution.
  4. Stop when no further rotational pair can be removed without creating multiple solutions.

The result is a pair-minimal puzzle — removing any remaining rotational pair would break uniqueness. This ensures every puzzle is as lean as possible while remaining solvable by logic alone.
