How to Solve Gattai Sudoku

A technique-by-technique guide to solving overlapping Sudoku puzzles, from basic singles to advanced patterns.

Every puzzle on this site can be solved by logic alone — no guessing required. The solver built into the site can walk you through any puzzle step by step, showing which technique was used and why. This guide explains each technique so you can recognise them in your own solving.

Tip: Open any puzzle, click Read solution, and use the step controls to follow along. The solver highlights the relevant cells and explains its reasoning for every deduction.

Getting started: entry modes

The puzzle board offers two entry modes:

  • Digit mode: Enter a single digit into a cell. The site does not maintain pencil marks for you in this mode — it's closest to pen-and-paper solving.
  • Snyder notation: Mark candidates in each cell as small corner digits. This is the recommended mode for most solvers. The site automatically shows centre marks for cells with only two candidates (a Snyder convention) and full notation for all other cells.

You can switch between modes at any time. Snyder notation is particularly powerful in Gattai because the overlapping region creates so many cross-grid candidate interactions.

Techniques by difficulty

Beginner techniques

  • Full House Score: 4 A cell that has only one remaining candidate in its entire 3×3 box. Fill it and move on. Common in the opening phase of Easy puzzles.
  • Naked Single Score: 4 A cell where only one digit is possible when you check its row, column, and box simultaneously. In the shared 6×6, this means checking two rows, two columns, and both grids' box constraints.
  • Hidden Single Score: 14 A digit that can only go in one cell of a given house, even though that cell still has other candidates. In Gattai, Hidden Singles in the shared 6×6 are especially common — a digit might be eliminated from most cells by Grid 1 constraints and from the rest by Grid 2.

Medium techniques

  • Intersections (Pointing & Claiming) Score: 30–60 When a candidate is restricted to one row or column within a 3×3 box, you can eliminate it from the rest of that row or column outside the box (and vice versa). In Gattai, the shared 6×6 creates unusual intersection patterns that span both grids.
  • Naked Pair / Naked Triple Score: 80–140 Two (or three) cells in the same house that contain the same two (or three) candidates. Those candidates can be eliminated from all other cells in that house. These are the first techniques that require active candidate scanning.
  • Hidden Pair / Hidden Triple Score: 80–140 Two (or three) candidates that only appear in the same two (or three) cells of a house. All other candidates can be eliminated from those cells. Hidden pairs are often easier to spot than naked pairs in dense grids.

Hard techniques

  • X-Wing Score: 150–250 A pattern where the same candidate appears in exactly two rows across the same two columns (or vice versa). The candidate can be eliminated from all other cells in those columns (or rows). X-Wings in Gattai can span the border between the two grids — a powerful pattern unique to the variant.
  • Swordfish Score: 250–350 An extension of the X-Wing involving three rows and three columns. Rare in Easy and Medium puzzles; the hallmark of a Hard-rated Gattai.
  • XY-Wing Score: 200–300 Three cells with two candidates each, arranged so one cell (the "pivot") sees the other two ("wings"). The digit common to both wings can be eliminated from any cell that sees both wings. XY-Wings work identically in Gattai but can span the grid boundary.
  • Single-Digit Patterns Score: 180–300 Patterns like Skyscraper, Two-String Kite, and Empty Rectangle that eliminate candidates by following a single digit across the board. These are especially effective in the shared 6×6.

Advanced techniques (Unfair and above)

  • Forcing Chains Score: 400+ A chain of implications: "if this cell is X, then that cell must be Y, which forces another cell to be Z, which creates a contradiction — therefore the original cell cannot be X."
  • Colouring Score: 500+ Assign alternating colours to conjugate pairs (cells where a digit must be in one of two positions). If the same colour appears twice in the same house, that colour can be eliminated.
  • ALS (Almost Locked Sets) Score: 600+ A group of n cells containing n+1 candidates, which interacts with another ALS to force an elimination. The most advanced human-grade technique in the rating system.

Strategy tips for Gattai

  1. Scan both grids from the start. Don't focus on Grid 1, then Grid 2. The overlap means a placement in one grid can instantly open up deductions in the other.
  2. Use the grid highlight buttons. Toggle "Grid 1" and "Grid 2" highlights to see which cells belong to which grid. The shared 6×6 is automatically shaded.
  3. Check the shared region first. A digit that resolves a shared cell eliminates candidates in both grids, making it the most valuable type of placement.
  4. Snyder notation is your friend. The site's Snyder mode auto-manages candidates. Let it handle the bookkeeping while you focus on spotting patterns.
  5. Walk through the solver. If you're stuck, click "Read solution" and step through one technique at a time. The solver shows exactly what it's looking at and why the deduction works.
